# Renderer implementation notes

Detail moved out of `CLAUDE.md` to keep it short (it's loaded into every Claude Code request).
Read the section for a subsystem before changing it. Not published: `specs/` isn't part of the docs site.

## Lighting

**Lighting** (`renderer/lighting/`, `Game.addLight()`): directional/point/ambient `BGE.Light`s on the game renderer shade `isShaded` drawables (billboards as camera-facing cut-outs, `Drawable.lightWrap` for wrapped light); with none, shading is camera-facing as before. `Renderer.updateLighting()` diffs a value signature each frame and bumps `lightingVersion`, which `SceneObject.lightingChangedThisDraw` turns into a relight: `draw()` calls `relight()` instead of `findCanvasPosition()` when only the lights changed, so projected geometry is kept (only a model overrides it, to reshade its faces); a model skips the redraw if no face's shaded color changed, and an impostor absorbs light changes within `modelImpostorMaxAngleDegrees`/`modelImpostorMaxLightChange` of its capture. Planes (`isShaded`) are tinted at the final blit, except a `tiledImage` plane with a ranged point light's pool in view: then all its lighting is drawn into the top-down bitmap (pools blended over a kept lit copy, so a lamp moving alone skips the texture warp), within `planeLightPoolMaxDriftPixels`/`planeLightPoolMaxLightChange`. Pools never switch off with quality, so adaptive quality can't make lights blink.

## Level walls

**Level walls** (`BGE.DrawableWalls`, `renderer/walls/`, `docs/drawables-and-scene-objects.md`): segments at any angle with their own foot/top heights (`BGE.createWallSegment()`); `BGE.DrawableGridWalls` is a `DrawableWalls` built from a `GridWallsMap` (`toSegments()`, rebuilt by `setMap()`, default `hideTolerance` a quarter cell). Drawn on a `Camera3d` as rotated `DrawTransformedObject` texture-column strips, `wallStripWidth` apart, in their own `walls` stage (after planes, before sorted objects). `BGE.WallsView` doesn't raycast: it walks the segments facing the camera front to back (`BGE.WallsBsp`, built by `DrawableWalls.new()`/`setSegments()`, never while drawing; nearest-point order lost pillars in front of long walls; subtrees outside the view are skipped by node bounds), projects each onto the strip fan, and keeps a per-ray elevation window that nearer walls close, so short walls show taller ones behind and a closed ray costs nothing more. Its cost is the walk and the strips, both proportional to visible strips (skipping closed rays measured no gain). Sorted objects (even culled ones) hidden by a wall are skipped and report `occluded`; `drawWorld*` overlays hide via `Renderer.isBehindWalls()`. `getDepth()` is where a ray closes, `getNearestDepth()` the nearest wall on it. Each segment's light is cached in buckets until the lights change (with no lights, shading follows the camera). One walls drawable per renderer. Roll ignored; pitch ~15° up to 35° down; wall tops not drawn; nothing below the lowest foot; no collision (use your grid). The grid raycaster was removed in #472 (segments matched or beat it on the crypt). The walk has three paths: level and unrolled (each ray's canvas column is fixed and a slope e sits on row `hh - focal * e`), pitched and unrolled (every strip on a ray lies on one canvas line through the vanishing point of uprights, so lean, cos/sin and the next ray's line are per ray, not per piece; widths are the distance to the next ray's line), and the general matrix path (roll; tests force it with `allowLevelPath = false`). Strips are drawn straight on the `roDraw2d` with the column and Y scale picked in the shade pass. `lodDepth` (from `wallLodDistanceFraction`) absorbs every other far strip into its neighbour (`pieceWide`; `isPointHidden()` and `redrawStrips()` check the ray to the right too). `recordSurfaces` records floor/ceiling slices as canvas rows in runs (floor in front of a wall is at its foot) and, unlike plain walls, closes the window's bottom at a recorded floor's far edge; `SceneObjectWalls.prepareView()` walks early and `render()` reuses it when the camera is unchanged. Measured on a Roku Ultra (floors benchmark, Medium, 4 rings turning): 115.8 ms a frame before these to 75 ms; the crypt maze level 36.6 to 44.5 fps.

## Freestanding walls

**Freestanding walls** (`BGE.DrawableWall`, #467): an upright quad between two floor points drawn as an oriented image, its texture tiled by `tileWidth`/`tileHeight` (`QuadColumnDrawer.drawTiledTo()`: columns wrap the texture in one pass, at least one slab per tile, bands along the column length; partial tiles cut with `getPart()`/`getRange()`). Occluder, shaded and two-sided by default; the owner must be `isStatic` for the BSP/occlusion. Cost is per wall and per visible column while the camera moves (`walls-benchmark` has switches per feature).

## Quad columns

**Quad columns** (`renderer/quadColumns/`, `Renderer.drawTexturedQuadTo()`, #447): draws a textured quad as projected `DrawTransformedObject` columns, each a whole power-of-two slab of texels (rows are a column region turned a quarter, no transposed copy). A quad's strip drawn mirrored (anchored at its far edge, e.g. a wall seen from behind) draws a slab from a flipped copy (`QuadColumnTexture.getMirrored()`, made once at rotation 0), or its middle texel alone when `canMirror` is off (text, redrawn in place). Model face strips do the same from the copy their texture shares (`TriangleColumnCuts.mirrorSource`, set by `Model3dTexture`). Sampling only one texel per slab looked fine on the canvas but striped in temp bitmaps (#459): on device, scaled regions draw into a bitmap without the neighbouring-texel bleed the screen shows. Column count comes from `quadColumnsPerTexel`/`quadMinPixelsPerColumn`, capped by `quadMaxAffineErrorPixels` (a slab is affine, so only depth change across the quad needs more; off at Ultra and for per-corner tints); `QuadColumnDrawer.maxColumnsPerFrame` caps draws per frame. Works on both cameras: a `Camera2d` projects orthographically (`QuadColumnDrawer.setOrthographicProjection`). Oriented/solid images, sprites, text, circles and filled rectangles draw this way (#449), unless the one-call rotated-rectangle fast path applies. Columns go straight to the canvas only while the object moves; a still one draws them once into its temp bitmap and blits that (redrawing columns every frame halved a still scene's fps). Sprites share column cuts via `SpriteFrames.getColumnTexture()`; rectangles tint a shared white texture. Textured model faces draw as strips cut to their UV triangle (`Renderer.drawTriangleColumnsTo()`, `BGE.TriangleColumnCuts` cached on `Model3dTexture`, #450), power-of-two texels wide, clipped along their length at the near plane; measured in rendererTest's `model-faces-benchmark`, and `camera2d-model` shows them on a `Camera2d`. A face columns can't draw (budget spent, degenerate UVs) is filled with its shaded color and clears `lastDrawWasFullyFresh`, so the drawing isn't reused. Solid faces fill straight to the canvas or temp bitmap with no per-face rasters (#452, `faces=solid`). The corner-pin / triangle-warp path, `TriangleCache` and per-face rasters were removed in #454. A model with an impostor (`DrawableModel.useImpostor`) draws columns straight to the canvas while its own transform changes and captures again once it stops; the impostor is rotate-scale only (the corner-pin impostor was removed in #451: slower than redrawing columns).

## Models

**Models** (`SceneObjectModel`): the faces' distinct vertices (deduplicated by coordinates, first 1201 faces) are built once per geometry version (`buildMesh()`); each frame the model moves they're transformed inline into world vectors the world faces share and mutate in place, with normals and bounds built inline. On a `Camera3d` each distinct vertex is projected once (`projectMeshFaces()`, the same arithmetic as `Camera3d.worldPointToCanvasPoint()`/`distanceFromCameraFront()`, so results match exactly), and its camera-space position is kept so textured faces draw through `Renderer.beginTriangleColumnsTo()`/`drawCameraTriangleColumnsTo()` with the projection set once per model. Canvas points are new each frame (impostor captures keep them). A face without 3 vertices, a `Camera2d`, and the per-face cluster path use the general code. Drawing may re-wind a canvas face's vertex array; use `faceIndex` and `meshCorners` for vertex order. Ultra, `model-faces-benchmark` spin: sphere (176 faces) 133 → 56 ms, 9 cubes 99 → 72 ms; what's left is mostly the ~40 µs per column strip.
